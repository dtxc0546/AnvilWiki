## What should you do when a tsunami appears?
Look at the threat, read the current prompt and choose a destination you can actually reach. The official Roblox description names tsunami as one possible idea, but it does not publish a wave timer, a guaranteed safe height or a universal hiding place. This guide provides a practice method rather than a tested map route.

## How do you avoid choosing a trap?
Before moving, inspect both your destination and its exit. A nearby structure may look useful but become difficult to leave when another word changes the arena. Prefer an observable route over a distant landmark you have never reached.

Check whether the wave direction is visible from your current position. Do not spend the entire warning searching for a spot described for another island layout. Use the [beginner guide](/guides/getting-started/) to practice reading a prompt and moving before the next effect appears.

## What changes when hazards overlap?
The developer explains that word effects accumulate. A route that worked against one threat may fail when the floor, visibility or nearby space also changes. Recheck the route after a new submission instead of assuming your first destination remains suitable.

The [lava guide](/guides/how-to-survive-lava/) gives a documented example of a floor hazard. Its instruction should not be turned into proof that the same climb defeats every tsunami. The [hazard checklist](/guides/idea-and-hazard-tips/) helps compare current threats.

## What should you record after a failed escape?
Note the exact displayed word, your starting position, the direction you moved, any other active effect and where the attempt ended. A screenshot of the warning can help separate a slow reaction from a blocked route.

Change one decision on the next comparable attempt. If you choose a different landmark and different equipment together, the outcome will be harder to interpret. This wiki has not verified a guaranteed tsunami immunity item, timer or height threshold.

## How can you compare two possible escapes?

Use three questions: can I reach it, can I see what happens there, and can I leave? A high destination can fail the first question if the approach is too long. A close destination can fail the third if it traps you against an obstacle. These are ways to assess your position, not a claim about a hidden safe-zone mechanic.

| Candidate | What to inspect | Reason to reconsider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nearby raised surface | Approach and visible landing | The first step is blocked |
| Distant tall structure | Travel route and intermediate exits | You cannot see a continuous route |
| Open ground away from the wave | Direction and turning space | Another effect crosses that space |
| A route used by other players | Whether it is reachable from your position | Their starting point differs from yours |

Do the comparison while you still have options. Watching another player reach a platform does not prove that you can repeat it from a different side. If the prompt provides a specific action, that instruction is stronger evidence than a generic route suggestion from this guide.

## What is a useful practice scenario?

Imagine a wave warning appears while you are near a short raised surface and can see a much taller structure farther away. The first decision is not simply which structure is higher. Inspect the approach to each and choose the route you can carry out before the threat reaches you. This is a hypothetical exercise, not a recording of a tested tsunami sequence.

If you reach the nearer surface, continue observing instead of treating the move as a completed solution. A new word may affect that surface, the approach or your next exit. If the route fails, note whether the failure came during travel, the climb or waiting at the destination. Each suggests a different comparison on the next attempt.

## How should you build a personal route note?

Describe landmarks in plain language and record the current island or map label if one is shown. Include the visible cue, start, approach, landing and exit. A useful note can be short: started beside one landmark, tried a particular approach, lost access when another effect appeared. Avoid claiming the approach is safe until you have repeatable evidence.

When comparing attempts, keep your equipment visible in a screenshot if it affects movement. Do not infer an equipment bonus from a different starting position or a new map. If you are unsure whether two waves behaved the same way, label the comparison uncertain.

## What should you avoid assuming from an old video?

A video may show a different layout, active effect combination or item setup. Check its date and what is actually visible. A successful jump is evidence that the player made that jump in that situation; it does not establish the required height or the wave’s universal collision rules.

Use the video to identify a question you can test, such as whether an approach remains open. Return to your current game prompt for the actual decision. That keeps a useful reference from becoming a misleading promise of immunity.
