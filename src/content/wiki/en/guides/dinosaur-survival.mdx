## How should you react to a dinosaur event?
Make space, identify where the threat is moving and keep another route available. Dinosaur is named in the official experience description. That description does not establish its movement speed, target selection or a particular item that always defeats it.

Treat the following as suggested practice. We have not completed a controlled dinosaur test, so no route here is presented as a guaranteed survival method.

## What should you watch before choosing a route?
Look for the threat's current location, the space between you and it, and an exit that remains visible. A movement you observed once does not prove that it always pursues the nearest player or follows the same path. Keep observations separate from those explanations.

Avoid choosing a narrow destination solely because it looks sheltered. It may restrict your next move. The [general hazard guide](/guides/idea-and-hazard-tips/) explains why a second exit matters when word effects stack.

## Can an item solve the encounter?
Read your actual item description and equipped loadout. This wiki does not have a verified item-versus-dinosaur counter table. A promotional item name, a spin reward or another player's clip is not enough to establish immunity.

Use [items and loadouts](/guides/items-and-loadouts/) to inspect your setup before the next round. If you change an item for a comparison, keep the rest of your plan similar and record what the game actually shows.

## How do you learn from an unsuccessful round?
Write down whether you were caught while crossing open space, turning around, climbing or waiting in one place. Add the other words already active. These details make the next practice goal specific: leave earlier, keep the camera on the threat, or choose a less restrictive route.

Do not turn one successful escape into a claim that a particular landmark is always safe. The [words and effects reference](/guides/words-and-effects/) shows how different inputs can change available routes.

## What is confirmed by the source?
The [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/77900175935766/Survive-Your-Ideas) confirms dinosaur as an example event and describes the last-survivor objective. Source reviewed November 15, 2026. Exact behavior needs observation in the current build.

## How do you separate a chase from a crossing path?

Watch more than one moment before assigning a targeting rule. A dinosaur moving toward you could be following a route, reacting to another player, or pursuing you; a brief encounter alone does not settle which. You still need to move when threatened, but your later notes can preserve that uncertainty.

| Observation | Reasonable note | Unsupported conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The threat turned near another player | It changed direction at that location | It always targets the closest player |
| You escaped behind an obstacle | The escape worked on that attempt | The obstacle grants immunity |
| An item was equipped during survival | The item was present | That item caused the successful escape |
| You were caught after turning | Contact followed the turn | Turning always triggers an attack |

This distinction matters because a wrong explanation can produce a repeat mistake. If you assume a single obstacle is an immunity spot, you may stop watching other effects. A precise observation lets you use the successful route while continuing to check whether conditions changed.

## How should you handle a route with limited exits?

Before entering a narrow area, identify where you can turn next. If you cannot see the end, do not treat it as shelter simply because the entrance looks protected. Compare the open space around you with the risk of losing visibility.

Suppose one path leads around a structure and another ends behind it. The path around may preserve a second direction, while the dead end commits you to waiting or reversing. This is an example of route assessment, not a verified dinosaur collision rule. The current layout and other active hazards determine whether either choice is usable.

## What should an equipment comparison record?

Write down the item name exactly as shown, its description, when you used it and the visible result. If a cooldown or restriction is displayed, record that too. Do not add a number based on another game's similar item.

Repeat a comparable movement problem before concluding that the item solved it. If the dinosaur did not approach on the second attempt, the comparison did not test the same escape. You can still record the item’s visible behavior, but label the encounter outcome separately.

## How can you make the next attempt more useful?

Choose one question you can answer from the screen. Did I notice the threat before choosing a path? Could I see an exit after the turn? Did another effect close the destination? These questions produce observations even when you lose, unlike an ambitious goal of discovering the best counter in one round.

Keep the answer brief and change one decision. Earlier awareness, a clearer exit or a more deliberate item test are separate practice goals. Combine them only after you can tell which one was missing in the original attempt.
