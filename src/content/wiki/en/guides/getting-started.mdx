## What happens in a round?

Players take writing turns and enter ideas that can become hazards in the arena. Your job as a survivor is to react to what appears. Start by watching where the new threat goes and choosing an open route. Check that route again whenever another idea takes effect.

## What should you write on your first turn?

Start with one recognizable idea so you can tell what changes after you submit it. Before your turn, look for open space and notice where the other players are moving. Once your idea appears, watch the result before committing to a route.

## How do you survive when another hazard appears?

Look for the threat that could reach you first, then move toward an area with room to change direction. If you run toward the edge of the arena, check where you can turn next. A route that worked against one hazard can close when a second one appears.

Read [idea and hazard tips](/guides/idea-and-hazard-tips/) for a checklist you can use mid-round, or return to the [guides index](/guides/) or the [wiki FAQ](/faq/).

## What should you do before your first match?

Watch the lobby timer and use the wait to learn where the main menus are. A [recorded lobby walkthrough](https://games.resuhive.com/survive-your-ideas/guide) shows Items, Index, and Meta Words. You can start by learning the match loop and looking for escape routes, then explore the menus once you understand what gives you trouble.

| Screen cue | What to do | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lobby countdown | Finish checking menus before the match begins | Enter the arena ready to move |
| Writer prompt | Open the prompt and submit one clear word | Make the result easier to recognize |
| Hazard announcement | Read the instruction, then look at the arena | Match your movement to the actual danger |
| Next writer countdown | Check your position before another word arrives | Keep an escape route open |

## How should you practice in your first three matches?

Give yourself a different goal in each match. First, recognize the writer prompt and hazard announcement. Next, practice keeping an escape route open as the arena changes. In your third match, look back at what eliminated you and change one decision instead of trying a completely different strategy.

| Match | Practice goal | How to tell it worked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Read the cue when a word appears | You can explain why you moved |
| Second | Keep a route open after moving | You still have space to change direction |
| Third | Fix one repeat mistake | You recognize the same situation earlier |

If the floor turns to lava, recorded gameplay shows an instruction to climb. Check what you can reach from your current position before following another player. A platform that looks close may take longer to reach if you have to go around an obstacle.

If you are eliminated, note the last safe place you saw and when you stopped being able to reach it. That is a more specific thing to practice than simply trying to survive longer.

For a worked example, see [how to survive lava](/guides/how-to-survive-lava/).

## How should you prepare a route before the next word?

Pick one reachable destination and one way to leave it while you still have time to look around. A route is more useful when you know how to get onto it and where it leads. Recheck both parts as soon as a new word changes the arena.

Start from where your character is standing. Look for a path you can follow without running into a blind corner. If you choose a raised spot, identify the first step that gets you there. Seeing a high platform is only the start. You still need an approach, a way up, and somewhere to stand after landing.

Then consider what you would do if your preferred path closed. An alternate route can be as simple as an open direction you can turn toward. You do not need to memorize every obstacle. You are trying to avoid committing to a route that leaves you with one possible move.

| Place you are considering | Question to ask before moving |
| --- | --- |
| A high platform | Can I see the approach and the landing? |
| An open stretch of ground | Can I turn if something crosses it? |
| The edge of the arena | Where can I move next from there? |
| A crowded route | Is there another approach to the same destination? |

## What should you do if the floor becomes lava?

Read the warning and look for the closest surface you can get onto from where you stand. In a [recorded match](https://games.resuhive.com/survive-your-ideas/guide), the LAVA cue told players to leave the floor and climb. A nearby foothold with a visible landing gives you a route you can act on quickly.

Imagine that you can see a tall structure in the distance and a shorter raised surface beside you. The tall structure might eventually give you more room, but you have to reach it first. Check the shorter route while it is still available. Once you have climbed, look around again and decide where you could go if another effect appears.

If an attempted jump fails, look at why it failed before repeating it. You may have approached from a blocked side or aimed for a landing you could not see. The [lava survival guide](/guides/how-to-survive-lava/) has a more detailed route checklist and examples for players who start in different parts of the arena.

## How can you practice when you are not picked to write?

Use someone else's writing turn to practice reading the cue and finding a route. Notice what the announcement says, what actually changes around your character, and how much space you have left. Those observations are useful when your own turn comes because they show which situations are hard to handle.

Try predicting one consequence before moving. For example, if you see a cue that tells you to climb, point your camera toward a nearby raised surface and ask yourself how you would get there. Watch what the other players do, then compare their route with the one you can reach from your position.

You can also compare two examples in the [words and effects guide](/guides/words-and-effects/). A word that makes the floor hazardous and one that creates a structure ask you to solve different problems. Pay attention to the terrain that exists in your match rather than memorizing a route from someone else's recording.

## When should you explore Items, Index, and Meta Words?

Explore those menus while you are in the lobby and have time to read them. Start with whichever menu answers a question you brought back from a match. If you struggled to reach high ground, the Items screen is a sensible place to look for a description you can test.

Use the [items and loadouts guide](/guides/items-and-loadouts/) if you are trying to match gear to a specific problem. Pick one item and one thing to observe. For instance, note whether it helped you reach a landing, escape a blocked route, or recover after a mistake. This makes it easier to judge what helped when the next match has a different layout.

If you are still missing the writer prompt or the hazard warning, spend another match practicing those cues. You can return to the menus once you can describe what happened in the arena and what you want to do differently.
