## What happens in a round?

Players take writing turns and enter ideas that can become hazards in the arena. Your job as a survivor is to react to what appears. Start by watching where the new threat goes and choosing an open route. Check that route again whenever another idea takes effect.

## What should you write on your first turn?

Start with one recognizable idea so you can tell what changes after you submit it. Before your turn, look for open space and notice where the other players are moving. Once your idea appears, watch the result before committing to a route.

## How do you survive when another hazard appears?

Look for the threat that could reach you first, then move toward an area with room to change direction. If you run toward the edge of the arena, check where you can turn next. A route that worked against one hazard can close when a second one appears.

Read [idea and hazard tips](/guides/idea-and-hazard-tips/) for a checklist you can use mid-round, or return to the [guides index](/guides/) or the [wiki FAQ](/faq/).

## What should you do before your first match?

Watch the lobby timer and use the wait to learn where the main menus are. A [recorded lobby walkthrough](https://games.resuhive.com/survive-your-ideas/guide) shows Items, Index, and Meta Words. You can start by learning the match loop and looking for escape routes, then explore the menus once you understand what gives you trouble.

| Screen cue | What to do | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lobby countdown | Finish checking menus before the match begins | Enter the arena ready to move |
| Writer prompt | Open the prompt and submit one clear word | Make the result easier to recognize |
| Hazard announcement | Read the instruction, then look at the arena | Match your movement to the actual danger |
| Next writer countdown | Check your position before another word arrives | Keep an escape route open |

## How should you practice in your first three matches?

Give yourself a different goal in each match. First, recognize the writer prompt and hazard announcement. Next, practice keeping an escape route open as the arena changes. In your third match, look back at what eliminated you and change one decision instead of trying a completely different strategy.

| Match | Practice goal | How to tell it worked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Read the cue when a word appears | You can explain why you moved |
| Second | Keep a route open after moving | You still have space to change direction |
| Third | Fix one repeat mistake | You recognize the same situation earlier |

If the floor turns to lava, recorded gameplay shows an instruction to climb. Check what you can reach from your current position before following another player. A platform that looks close may take longer to reach if you have to go around an obstacle.

If you are eliminated, note the last safe place you saw and when you stopped being able to reach it. That is a more specific thing to practice than simply trying to survive longer.

For a worked example, see [how to survive lava](/guides/how-to-survive-lava/).
