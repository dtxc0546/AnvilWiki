## What should you review after elimination?
Identify the last decision you could reasonably change. The useful question is specific: did you leave too late, choose a blocked route, overlook the warning or misunderstand the active effect? This page offers a review worksheet rather than a claim about an unverified respawn or spectator menu.

The developer describes a last-survivor objective and accumulating effects. Those conditions make a brief post-round note useful even when the exact combination does not repeat.

## How do you write a useful round note?
Record the visible threat, your position, your chosen destination and the moment the route failed. Add an item only if it was relevant to the action you attempted. Keep what you saw separate from what you think caused it.

For example, a route becoming blocked is an observation. Concluding that a specific word always blocks that route needs more evidence. Use the [word-effect reference](/guides/words-and-effects/) to compare documented examples with your own notes.

## Should you change your loadout immediately?
Only if the problem you identified concerns an item or a missing capability you can verify. Buying or swapping equipment after every loss can hide a repeated positioning error. Read the actual description and compare one change at a time.

The [items guide](/guides/items-and-loadouts/) helps inspect the menu. The [codes page](/guides/survive-your-ideas-codes/) lists reported reward opportunities separately; a spin does not promise the item you want or a guaranteed win.

## What should your next practice goal be?
Pick one observable behavior: read the warning before moving, keep a second exit, or leave a crowded destination earlier. Avoid a vague goal such as surviving everything. One small goal makes it easier to recognize improvement even when another hazard ends the attempt.

For a route review, use [idea and hazard tips](/guides/idea-and-hazard-tips/). For a long-term objective, check [badges](/guides/badges/) against their current requirements rather than guessing from a round result.

## What remains unverified?
This wiki has not established a universal recovery timer, respawn cost or streak-reset rule for every build. The [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/77900175935766/Survive-Your-Ideas) is the source for the broad game loop, reviewed November 15, 2026.

## How can you tell whether the problem was awareness or movement?

Reconstruct the sequence in order. When did you first see the warning, when did you choose a destination, and when did you begin moving? If the destination was already unreachable by the time you noticed it, practice earlier awareness. If you noticed the cue but chose a blocked approach, practice route inspection.

| Last observable problem | Next practice question | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warning noticed late | Can I read the next cue promptly? | The cue and my position |
| Approach blocked | Can I see the entire first part of the route? | Start and obstacle |
| Landing failed | Was the landing visible before I committed? | Approach angle |
| Destination became unsafe | Did I recheck after the next word? | Order of active effects |
| Item did not do what I expected | What did its description actually promise? | Item text and response |

These are practice categories, not a diagnosis made from the final elimination screen. If you did not observe the relevant moment, mark it unknown. Guessing a cause too confidently can lead you to practice the wrong thing.

## What would a worked review look like?

Imagine that you climbed to avoid a floor hazard, stayed on the platform and then lost access to an exit after another effect appeared. A useful review identifies the second announcement as the moment to reassess. It does not conclude that climbing was always wrong.

Your next practice goal could be to check the ground and another exit immediately after a new cue. Keep the rest of your approach similar so you can tell whether the extra check helped. This is a hypothetical review example; it is not a confirmed sequence from a tested match.

## How do you avoid confusing progress with results?

A longer survival time can be encouraging, but it does not by itself identify why the attempt improved. Different words and player choices change the difficulty. Look for the behavior you chose to practice: did you notice the prompt, find a second exit or read the item description before using it?

Likewise, a shorter round does not prove your adjustment failed. You may have handled one decision better and then encountered an unfamiliar problem. Record both separately. This gives you a more useful learning history than treating every round as a single pass or fail.

## What belongs in a correction sent to this wiki?

Include the page URL, the statement that differs from your game, the approximate date and the visible evidence. A cropped screenshot of the prompt or menu can be more useful than a claim that the guide is wrong. Explain whether you observed the result once or repeated it.

Use the site’s [contact page](/contact/) and remove private account information. The wiki cannot restore a streak or change a game result. A report can help correct a guide when it identifies a specific, supportable difference.

## When should you stop repeating the same experiment?

When its starting conditions no longer match, when you cannot observe the result clearly, or when another question is more basic. If you are unsure what the warning means, first learn that cue. If the item behavior is unclear, inspect its description. A useful review ends with one manageable next step, not an obligation to recreate the same loss indefinitely.
